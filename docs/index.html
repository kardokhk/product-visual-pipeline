Quality loop

Every candidate is scored automatically and approved by a person. In this run review caught:

  • edits that duplicated the product (both first-pass personalised labels added a second bottle; the object-count check flagged both, and a revised instruction fixed all four new candidates),
  • edits that removed elements the instruction said to keep (a sink, a doorway),
  • colour drift under warm scene light.

After the first pass, warm scene light was pulling product colours off-brand. The scene template was revised with an explicit colour lock and all eight product scenes were rerun with the same seeds. Product colour error fell in 7 of 8 scenes (paired t(7) = -2.63, P = 0.03), with no detectable change in identity or prompt match. The scene is the unit of analysis (the two seeds per scene are averaged); the comparison was designed after reviewing the first pass and is exploratory.

The checks rank and flag; they do not replace review. In this run the count check missed two overlapping duplicates (a single shoe turned into a pair, caught at a second review and fixed the same way) and flagged three wet-pavement reflections as second objects.

How it works

Three plain-text files drive every run: a brand kit (product description, exact label text, palette, mood), a template library with named slots, and a campaign file listing what to make. A run expands these into written prompts, generates several seeded candidates per item on GPU, scores them, and waits for a person to approve the picks before the next stage. Code: https://github.com/kardokhk/product-visual-pipeline.

This run: 14 master and before candidates (median 108 s each), 61 edit candidates (median 120 s), 16 video clips (median 6 min), on single NVIDIA A100 40 GB GPUs.
